Pluck a rubber band and you can see it blur. That blur is the band moving back and forth too quickly to follow, and the hum you hear is that movement reaching your ear. This is all a sound is: something **vibrating**, which means moving back and forth around its resting place.

Try it on a string. Drag anywhere on the string below to pull it aside, then let go.

```widget
type: string-sim
mode: pluck
note: A2
```

The string snaps back, overshoots, comes back again, and keeps going. Each swing is a little smaller than the last, which is why the sound fades. Stop the string and the sound stops with it.

## From the string to your ear

A moving string pushes on the air beside it. That air pushes on the air next to it, and the push spreads outward in every direction, a little like ripples on a pond. Nothing travels from the string to you except that disturbance. When it reaches your eardrum, the eardrum is pushed back and forth in the same pattern, and you hear it.

> Sound is vibration. A vibrating object pushes the air, the push travels as a wave, and your ear picks up the wave.

## Drawing a vibration

We can draw that back-and-forth movement as a line: time runs left to right, and the line goes up and down as the air is pushed and pulled. This picture is called a **waveform**. Play any note on the keyboard below and watch the display.

```widget
type: scope
show: wave
```

```widget
type: keyboard
range: [C3, C5]
labels: none
```

Every note draws a repeating wiggle. Low keys draw wide, slow wiggles and high keys draw tight, fast ones. The next lesson is about exactly that difference.
