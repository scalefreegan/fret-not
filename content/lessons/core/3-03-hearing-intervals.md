Naming intervals on paper is half the skill. The other half is knowing them by sound, so that you can hear a tune and know what it is doing. This takes regular, short practice rather than cleverness, and it is the reason this concept will keep returning in your reviews.

Every interval has a character that stays the same whichever note it starts on. Play each of these, both one note after the other and together.

## Octave and fifth: open and hollow

The octave sounds like the same note again, higher. The perfect fifth is strong, open and stable.

```widget
type: interval
from: C4
to: G4
```

The first leap of "Twinkle, Twinkle, Little Star" (the second "twinkle") is a perfect fifth up.

```widget
type: melody
title: Twinkle, Twinkle, Little Star (traditional French tune), opening
notes: "C4:q C4:q G4:q G4:q | A4:q A4:q G4:h"
tempo: 100
```

## Perfect fourth: a call to attention

The fourth is a little smaller than the fifth and feels like a lift. "Amazing Grace" begins with one: "A-**ma**-".

```widget
type: melody
title: Amazing Grace (traditional American hymn tune), opening
notes: "r:h D4:q | G4:h B4:e G4:e | B4:h A4:q | G4:h."
time: 3/4
key: G
tempo: 84
```

## Major and minor thirds: bright and dark

These two are only a half step different in size, but the mood changes completely. The major third sounds bright and settled. The minor third sounds darker, more serious or sad. This single difference is what makes major and minor chords, later in this level.

Switch the top note between E and E♭ and listen with the notes played together.

```widget
type: interval
from: C4
to: E4
```

## Seconds: steps

The major second (a whole step) is the ordinary step of a scale. "Frère Jacques" starts with two of them going up. The minor second (a half step) is tighter and more tense.

```widget
type: melody
title: Frère Jacques (traditional French round), opening
notes: "C4:q D4:q E4:q C4:q | C4:q D4:q E4:q C4:q"
tempo: 100
```

> Tie each interval to the opening of a tune you know well. When you hear the interval, the tune will come to mind.

Useful anchors, all going upward: **major second**, Frère Jacques. **Major third**, the first two notes of "Oh, When the Saints". **Perfect fourth**, Amazing Grace. **Perfect fifth**, Twinkle, Twinkle. **Octave**, the same note, higher.

Do not expect to get these all right at once. Start with the pairs in the exercises, which are chosen to be easy to tell apart, and let the reviews bring the harder ones back.
