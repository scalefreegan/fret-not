Low notes, such as a pianist's left hand, a bass guitar or a cello, are written in the **bass clef**. Its two dots sit either side of line 4, and that line is the F below middle C.

The bass staff works exactly like the treble staff: line, space, line, space, one letter per step. Only the names attached to the positions are different.

## The lines

From the bottom, the five lines are **G, B, D, F, A**. Click each one and hear how much lower these are than the treble notes.

```widget
type: staff
clef: bass
notes: [G2, B2, D3, F3, A3]
labels: true
```

A traditional phrase is "**G**ood **B**oys **D**o **F**ine **A**lways".

## The spaces

From the bottom, the four spaces are **A, C, E, G**: "**A**ll **C**ows **E**at **G**rass".

```widget
type: staff
clef: bass
notes: [A2, C3, E3, G3]
labels: true
```

> Bass lines: G B D F A. Bass spaces: A C E G.

## A shortcut from the treble clef

Every position in the bass clef is named **two letters later** than the same position in the treble clef. The bottom line is E in treble and G in bass. The top line is F in treble and A in bass. If you ever blank on a bass note, read it as treble and move on two letters.

Good landmarks in the bass clef are **F** on line 4 (between the dots), **C** in space 2, and **G** on the bottom line.
