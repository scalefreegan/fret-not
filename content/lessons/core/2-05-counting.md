To play a rhythm accurately, musicians **count** it. You already count the beats: "1 2 3 4". To place notes that fall between beats you need something to say there too.

## Eighth notes: "and"

Two eighth notes share one beat. The first lands on the beat. The second lands exactly halfway to the next beat, and is counted "**and**", written **&**.

So a bar of eighth notes in 4/4 is counted "1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &". Play this and say the counting written under the notes out loud.

```widget
type: rhythm
pattern: "q q q q | e e e e e e e e | q e e q e e | e e q e e q"
tempo: 72
```

Keep saying "1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &" evenly even when you are playing quarter notes. The "ands" are still there. You just don't play on them.

## Dotted notes

A **dot** after a note makes it longer by half of its own length.

- A half note is 2 beats. A **dotted half note** is 2 + 1 = **3 beats**.
- A quarter note is 1 beat. A **dotted quarter note** is 1 + ½ = **1½ beats**.

A dotted half note fills a whole bar of 3/4. A dotted quarter is usually followed by a single eighth note, which fills in the missing half beat: together they make 2 beats.

> A dot adds half of the note's value. Dotted half = 3 beats. Dotted quarter = 1½ beats.

Listen to the dotted quarter in the last bar of this line: long, then a quick short note just before the long final note. Count "1 (2) & 3" through that bar: the eighth note lands on the "and" of beat 2.
