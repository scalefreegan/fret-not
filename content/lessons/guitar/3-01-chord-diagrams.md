Guitar chords are usually shown as a **chord diagram** (also called a chord box): a small picture of the top few frets of the fretboard with dots where your fingers go.

Imagine the guitar standing upright, facing you.

- The **six vertical lines** are the strings. The line on the **far left is string 6**, the thick low E string. The line on the far right is string 1, the thin high E string.
- The **horizontal lines** are the frets. The thick line at the top is the **nut**, the end of the fretboard. The first box down is fret 1, the next is fret 2, and so on.
- A **dot** means: press this string in this fret.
- An **O** above a string means play it **open**.
- An **X** above a string means **do not play** it.

Here is the diagram for E minor, one of the easiest chords there is. Click it to hear it strummed.

Read it string by string from the left: string 6 open, string 5 at fret 2, string 4 at fret 2, strings 3, 2 and 1 open. Two fingers, six strings sounding.


Every one of those six notes is an E, a G or a B: the three notes of the E minor triad, repeated in different octaves.

> A chord diagram is the fretboard seen upright. Left line = low E string. Dots = fingers. O = open. X = leave out.

## Finger numbers

Numbers in or under the dots say which finger to use: **1** index, **2** middle, **3** ring, **4** little finger. The thumb stays behind the neck and has no number. For E minor the usual choice is finger 2 on string 5 and finger 3 on string 4.

## A diagram with an X

Now A minor. Compare it with E minor.

```widget
type: chord-diagram
chords: [Em, Am]
```

A minor has an **X** over string 6: the low E string is left out, and the strum starts from string 5. That is because the lowest note of a chord sounds best when it is the root, and string 5 open is an A.

String by string, A minor is: X, open, fret 2, fret 2, fret 1, open.

## Diagram or tab?

Tab shows music moving through time. A chord diagram shows one frozen hand shape. A song sheet typically prints the diagrams once at the top and then just writes the chord symbols, Em or Am, above the words.
