**Open chords** are chord shapes played in the first three frets that include at least one open string. They ring fully, they are the first chords every guitarist learns, and eight of them are enough for thousands of folk, pop and rock songs.

## Three notes, six strings

A triad has three notes, but a guitar chord uses four, five or six strings. The extra strings simply **repeat notes of the triad in other octaves**. Look at G major on the fretboard:

```widget
type: chord
symbol: G
views: [fretboard, diagram, staff]
```

From string 6 upward the notes are G, B, D, G, B, G. Only G, B and D, the G major triad, with the root three times.

This is why the X marks matter. In C major, string 6 is left out because its open note, E, would put the third of the chord at the very bottom and muddy it. The strum starts on string 5, where fret 3 is the root, C.

```widget
type: chord
symbol: C
views: [fretboard, diagram]
```

> An open chord is a triad with some of its notes doubled across the strings. The lowest string played is usually the root.

## Major and minor are one finger apart

In the core lesson on triads, the only difference between a major and a minor triad was the third, moved by one half step. One half step on the guitar is one fret, so the major and minor versions of a chord differ by **one fret on one string**.

Compare each pair:

```widget
type: chord-diagram
chords: [E, Em, A, Am, D, Dm]
```

- **E → Em**: string 3 goes from fret 1 (G♯) to open (G).
- **A → Am**: string 2 goes from fret 2 (C♯) to fret 1 (C).
- **D → Dm**: string 1 goes from fret 2 (F♯) to fret 1 (F).

In each case the string that changes is the one playing the third of the chord.
